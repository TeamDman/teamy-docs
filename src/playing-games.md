# Playing games

Choose what a session is for before optimizing it. Enjoyment, learning, exploration and competition are valid goals, and they can call for different decisions.

## State the objective

Compare these objectives:

| Objective | Useful feedback | A reasonable experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn a mechanic | Recognized situations and successful attempts | Practice a small repeatable scenario |
| Explore a world | New areas and discoveries | Follow an unfamiliar route |
| Improve competitive results | Performance across comparable matches | Change one tactic for a set of matches |
| Spend time with friends | The group's experience | Choose a mode the group enjoys |

Do not assume that a score measures the whole experience. A tactic may improve a statistic while making the session less enjoyable.

## Review the decision, not only the outcome

An outcome can be affected by uncertainty. Record what was known when making the choice, the alternative considered and what would change the next attempt.

A short review might read:

> I tried the same route three times. It was fast when uncontested, but I had no recovery route when blocked. Next session I will practice a fallback before comparing times again.

This makes the next experiment concrete without turning every session into paperwork.

## Keep experiments reversible

Prefer a change that can be tried and undone: a practice routine, a key binding or a tactical choice. Note the original setting before changing it. Treat downloads, account access and purchases as separate decisions requiring their own evidence and scope.

The same language of [goals, constraints and feedback](glossary.md) can support software work, while the session's actual purpose determines what success means.
